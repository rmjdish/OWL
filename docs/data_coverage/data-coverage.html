OWL and the NSHD Showcase currently provide access to around 28,486 variables — those that have been cleaned and enhanced as part of the Metadata Enhancement Project. The full NSHD repository contains approximately 35,000+ variables; the remaining ~6,514 are available on Condor. New variables are added to OWL and the Showcase periodically as each questionnaire is processed and deposited. See below for more details for what is available on this platform.

The 28,486 variables shown here cover 42 collection years spanning 1946 to 2025, across 9 topic areas and 50+ data collection forms. The charts below break these down in detail.

Variables by topic area

Variables are organised into 9 top-level topic areas. Questionnaire data (9,004) and Diet diaries (4,888) make up the largest share, followed by Specialist questionnaire data, Biological samples, and Sub-studies.

Single-sweep vs multi-sweep Field IDs

Of the 22,610 unique Showcase Field IDs, 10% were collected at more than one time point with identical question text and coding. These multi-sweep fields are the ones where the NSHD Showcase groups instances together for direct longitudinal comparison.

Single-sweep: 20,258 (90%). Multi-sweep: 2,352 (10%).

2,352 Field IDs (10%)

Note: this chart groups sweeps by distinct collection year per Field ID. A small number of Field IDs were collected more than once within the same labelled year — for example, Covid wave 1 and wave 2 are both recorded under the year 2020, despite being separate questionnaires collected months apart — and are counted here as single-sweep. Because of this, the multi-sweep count above is slightly higher than the number of longitudinal fields shown on the Longitudinal Variables page, which counts sweeps individually rather than by distinct year.

Variables by life stage

The volume of variables reflects the intensity of data collection at each phase of life. The richest coverage is at ages 56–65 — corresponding to the major 2006–2010 clinic visit — followed by ages 46–55 and 66–75. Early-life coverage (birth to age 25) is smaller in scale but represents foundational measures collected across the study's first two decades.

Life stage counts listed in page text.

Variables by collection year

The chart shows variable counts for each collection year or period. The dominant peaks reflect the major intensive data collection sweeps — 2006-10 (8,581), 1999 (4,029), 1989 (2,680), 1982 (2,334). Early sweeps in the 1940s–70s have fewer variables, reflecting the simpler instruments used in those decades.
