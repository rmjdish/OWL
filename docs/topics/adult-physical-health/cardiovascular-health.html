Around one in two men and one in three women suffer a cardiovascular (CV) event sometime during life. Questions relating to CVD were asked in 1982, 1989, 1999, 2006–10, 2014–16, 2022–23 and 2025.

Blood pressure

Blood pressure was measured at ages 36, 43, 53, 60–64, 69 and 77. At 53 and 60–64 it was measured twice seated after 5 minutes rest. At 69, three times under the same conditions. At 77, twice standing (at 1 minute and 3 minutes after standing). Study members were also asked at each interview whether they had taken prescribed medicines for high blood pressure in the last year.

Heart trouble

As a child, school doctors recorded heart irregularities or murmurs at examinations in 1952, 1953, 1957 and 1961. In 1982 and 1989, study members were asked about heart trouble in themselves and their parents. From 1999 onward, heart attacks were separated from other heart trouble (valvular disease, aortic stenosis, ischaemic heart disease, palpitations, etc.). Longitudinal derived variables covering angina, high blood pressure, diabetes, heart failure, heart attack (MI) and stroke up to 2016 are available.

Angina

The Rose Angina battery was asked in 1989 and 1999 with three derived scales: Stage 1 (chest pain on exertion, relieved by rest), Stage 2 (chest pain at ordinary pace), and overall Rose Angina (sum of stages 1 and 2). Direct doctor-diagnosed angina questions were asked from 1999 onward.

Intermittent claudication

Diagnostic intermittent claudication questions were asked in 1999. Defined by: leg pain on walking, felt in the calf, not present at rest, not relieved whilst walking but relieved on standing still in less than 10 minutes.

Cardiological scans

As part of the 2006–10 data collection, wave 2 and 3 of Insight 46 and MyoFit46, cardiological scans were performed including ECG, Heart Rate Variability (HRV), Pulse Wave Velocity/Analysis (PWV/PWA), ECHO and IMT. These are specialist datasets — please search Skylark and contact us if you wish to use them.

CVD risk factors

At 53, 69 and 77 years, non-fasting venous blood samples were taken; at 60–64 a fasting venous blood sample was taken. Total cholesterol was measured by enzymatic CHOD–PAP. HDL cholesterol was measured using phosphotungstic Mg²⁺ precipitation. Triglycerides were measured using a glycerol/kinase POD linked reaction. HbA1c was analysed using the Tosoh A1C 2.2 Plus Analyzer. LDL cholesterol was calculated using the Friedwald formula: LDL = total cholesterol – HDL – 0.45 × triglycerides.
