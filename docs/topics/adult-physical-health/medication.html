Medication

BNF-categorised medication data collected at home visits and postal questionnaires from age 31 to 79.

Data collection

Information on the type and number of medications taken regularly has been obtained from reports made at six home visits at ages 36, 43, 53, 60–64, 68–70 and 76–77, and from responses to postal questionnaires at ages 31, 60–64 and 79. Respondents were usually asked if they 'regularly take any medicines, pills or tablets (or have regular injections)?' and asked to provide the reason, duration, name and dosage of any medications taken.

The 2006–10 data collection was slightly different: the postal questionnaire (PQ) used the usual phrasing about regular taking of medications, whereas the home visit (HV) asked for details of medications taken in the past 24 hours. When overall derived measures were created, both PQ and HV data were combined with priority given to the HV (where the nurse was presented with medications by the study member, allowing more accurate reporting).

Coding and derived variables

Information given was converted into BNF-categorised codes and then into classes of drug. The entire NSHD medication data were thoroughly reviewed in 2017 and an ultimate set of derived measures created, listing drug use at various timepoints for a number of 'classes' of medical condition.

Data access

Due to the sensitive and possibly disclosive nature of medication data, particularly for rarer diseases, only summary variables are available. Particularly rare classes of drug may have been removed completely. Please contact the NSHD team for further information.
