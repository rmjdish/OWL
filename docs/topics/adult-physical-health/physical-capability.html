Physical capability

Objective tests of muscle strength and physical performance, plus self-reported activities of daily living, from age 36 to 79.

Physical capability refers to an individual's capacity to undertake the physical tasks of daily living. In the NSHD, muscle strength has been assessed through grip strength, and physical performance through standing balance and chair rise tests. Self-reported functional ability has also been assessed.

Muscle strength and physical performance

During visits in 1999, 2006–10, 2014–16 and 2022–23, nurses measured performance in three tests:

  • Grip strength — measured using an electronic dynamometer, twice in each hand (1999, 2006–10) or three recordings in each hand (2014–16, 2022–23).
  • Chair rises — time taken to rise from a chair and sit back down 10 times as fast as possible.
  • Standing balance — time (up to 30 seconds) standing on one leg with eyes open, and also with eyes closed.

In 1999 (age 53), 202 study participants at five clinics also had leg extensor power measured.

In 2006–10, a timed "get up and go" test was added (rise from chair, walk 3m, turn, walk back, sit). In 2014–16 and 2022–23 this was adapted for home use (walking distance shortened to 2.44m/8ft, without sitting/standing). Walking speed (m/sec) has been derived. Composite summary performance scores are available.

Activities of daily living (ADLs)

ADL questions were first asked in 1982 (age 36). At ages 43, 53, 60–64 and 68–70, the OPCS 1988 disability survey criteria were used. Main ADL categories:

  • Locomotor activities (walking a quarter mile, going up/down stairs, bending, keeping balance)
  • Using arms to reach or stretch for things
  • Using hands to hold, grip or turn things
  • Personal self-care activities

At age 43, a two-stage process was used: a general Disability Checklist (questionnaire B), followed by a detailed Disability Questionnaire (questionnaire C) if needed. Summary domain variables for locomotion, reaching & stretching, dexterity and personal care have been derived. A revised battery based on OPCS questions was asked at ages 77 and 79.
