Childhood mental health and personality

Early psychological wellbeing, behaviour and personality measures collected from age 6 through to age 16 years.

Early indicators (age 6–11)

Earliest indications of mental health are provided by maternal ratings of shyness and night-time sleep disturbance when study members were aged six years. At age nine years, mothers were asked about difficulties in the study member's relations with siblings and children at school. When study members were aged 11 years mothers were asked about presence or absence of habits (thumb sucking, nail biting, etc.) and again about sleep disturbance.

Teacher ratings (age 13–15)

Principal information on adolescent mental health was obtained from teacher ratings when study members were aged 13 and 15 years, using a forerunner of the Rutter A scale to identify conduct and emotional problems. Items for conduct problems refer to unpunctuality, restlessness, truancy, daydreaming, indiscipline, disobedience and lying. Items for emotional problems refer to anxiety, timidity, fearfulness, diffidence and avoidance of attention. Most items are graded from 1–3, roughly corresponding to absent, normal and severe. In addition, four items allow positive wellbeing ratings: very popular with other children, unusually happy and contented, makes friends extremely easily and extremely energetic, never tired. Summary measures at 15 years of antisocial behaviour and anxious behaviour have been derived.

Teachers made additional ratings at these ages for work style in the classroom, attitudes to schoolwork, and nervous habits.

Derived summary measures

Summary measures of childhood conduct and emotional problems were derived in 2009. These were created by deriving global measures for each from factor analysis, then dividing scores into absent, mild and severe based on established percentile cuts. For conduct problems these were 0–75%, 75–93% and 94% or higher, respectively; and for emotional problems these were 0–50%, 50–87% and 88% or higher, respectively. For both sets of problems the percentile cut for 'severe' was based on epidemiological evidence in order to capture problems of potential clinical significance. Further details of this methodology can be found in the Life chances report (Richards & Abbott, 2009) from the Centre for Mental Health.

Personality measures

Personality measures were obtained by self-completed questionnaire when study members were aged 13 and 16 years. At the former age the Pintner Aspects of Personality Inventory was used, which provides a measure of the child's personality along the dimensions of neuroticism and extroversion/introversion. At age 16 years study members answered six extroversion (E) and six neuroticism (N) items of the short Maudsley Personality Inventory. The E items refer to sociability, energy and activity orientation, and the N items address mood and distractibility. The MPI was administered again when study members were aged 26 years.

References: Pintner & Forlano (1938, 1939); Pintner, Loftus & Alster (1937); Eysenck, MPI (1958).
