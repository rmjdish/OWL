Parental bonding instrument (retrospective)

Retrospective measures of parental care and overprotection, collected from study members in adult life.

Overview

The Parental Bonding Instrument (PBI), developed by Parker, Tupling and Brown (1979), was administered retrospectively to study members in adult life(1989 Self completion). It asks participants to recall the attitudes and behaviours of each parent during their first 16 years of life.

The PBI produces two main subscale scores for each parent: Care (ranging from coldness and rejection to warmth and closeness) and Overprotection (ranging from autonomy-permitting to controlling and overprotective).

Scoring

The Care subscale consists of 12 items and the Overprotection subscale consists of 13 items, each scored on a 4-point Likert scale. Higher Care scores indicate greater warmth; higher Overprotection scores indicate greater control. Of the 25 standard items in the scale, one was omitted (item 4: Seemed emotionally cold to me), and five items were reworded from a negative direction to a positive direction (items 2, 8, 14, 18 and 24). Scores are calculated separately for the maternal and paternal figures.
