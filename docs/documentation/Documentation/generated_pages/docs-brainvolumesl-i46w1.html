Cross-sectional volumes

Baseline whole brain and hippocampal volumes were automatically segmented from T1 images at using MAPS and STEPS respectively (Leung et al., 2011; Cardoso et al., 2013). Images were visually checked for segmentation errors and manually edited by trained in-house personnel if needed. Baseline ventricular volumes were determined from T1 images using a semi-automated technique (Freeborough, Fox and Kitney, 1997). Grey matter (GM), white matter (WM), cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and total intracranial volumes (TIV) were calculated from T1 images using the tissues utility in Statistical Parametric Mapping (SPM) version 12 (Malone et al., 2015). All structural MRI scans underwent visual cross-sectional QC checking for motion, coverage and other artefacts. The ‘qc_okay’ variables indicates the cross-sectional QC result, where 1=pass, 0=fail. In the case of multiple passing scans (due to repeats acquired in the same imaging session) the ‘qc_use’ variable indicates the preferred scan that was used for analysis, 1=USED, 0=NOT USED.

BSI Methods

For subjects with T1 MRI data that passed cross-sectional quality control at both time-points, longitudinal changes in whole brain, ventricular and hippocampal volume were measured using the boundary shift integral (BSI) (Freeborough and Fox, 1997). The k-means normalised BSI was used to calculate whole brain atrophy following affine registration of T1 scan pairs and differential bias correction (DBC) (Leung et al., 2010b). Ventricular expansion was derived using affine whole-brain registration, followed by rigid registration using the ventricle regions, and calculation of BSI without DBC (Freeborough and Fox, 1997). Hippocampal atrophy was derived using affine whole-brain registration, followed by additional rigid registration focusing on the hippocampus and surrounding regions, with DBC and calculation of BSI using a double intensity window approach (Leung et al., 2010a). All registered scan pairs were reviewed by a trained team to ensure longitudinal continuity. Those with unacceptable differences in quality between scans were excluded (e.g., differences in motion or contrast, or registration issues such as stretch caused by positional differences between time-points). The ‘reg_rating’ variable specifies the outcome of registration QC: 1=good, 2=acceptable, 3=borderline, 4=fail, 5=fail due to non-neurodegenerative pathology influencing BSI. Where 1-3 values indicate QC pass.

Range checking

Range checking was performed on the complete dataset. These checks ensure that outliers are in line with accepted protocols and are not caused by segmentation or registration issues. Cross-sectional volumes Range checks were carried out on each region (brain, ventricle, left hippocampus, right hippocampus). The top 2%, the bottom 2% and a random 1% of all volumes for a region were checked. BSI measures For each region, the top 2%, the bottom 2% and a random 1% of all BSI values were checked, together with any scan-pairs that had significant discrepancies in directly (BSI) and indirectly (volume at follow-up minus volume at baseline) measured volume changes.
