Major brain disorders were determined by a consensus panel including consultant neurologists and study clinicians who had reviewed the participants. Decisions were made based on self-reported diagnosis (diagnosis1, other1, diagnosis2, other2), radiological reads (infarction, haemorrhage, lesion, aneurysm, colloidCyst, hydrocephalus, sinusDisease, other, comments, flaggedForReview) and clinician opinion. Participants were classified as having a major brain disorder if they had a clinical or radiological diagnosis of a significant neurological or psychiatric condition. These included but were not limited to: Neurodegenerative disorders Multiple sclerosis Epilepsy requiring active treatment Psychiatric disorder requiring hospital admission, anti-psychotic medication, or electroconvulsive therapy Major neurosurgery Clinical or radiological (cortical) stroke
