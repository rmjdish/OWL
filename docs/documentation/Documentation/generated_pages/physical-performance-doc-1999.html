Physical performance at age 53 years

During the home visit in 1999 nurses measured study participants’ performance in three tests of physical function: chair rises; standing balance; grip strength. 202 study participants also attended one of five clinics where leg extensor power was measured.

These variables are: SEX, INF, CHRST, CHRSTF, CHRSTRP, CHRST53G, LEGBC, LEGBP, LGLEGBC, LGLEG53G, GRIP53CF, GRIP53X, GRIP53G, CONTSUM, CONTLOW, CONTHIGH, CONTMID, LGXMAX, CLINIC, CLIPAR, LGXMAXG. Unless otherwise stated these variables are continuous.

Chair rises

was measured as the time taken in seconds to rise from a sitting to standing position and then sit down again ten times

Raw variable CHRST – Chair rise time recorded (seconds)

Derived variables (please note details of corrections that need to be made to CHRSTF and CHRSTRP (as outlined in the description of CHRSTFC and CHRSTRPC) CHRSTF – Chair rise time (seconds) – those with a value for CHRST of < 5 seconds, 888 or 999 coded as missing (this variable should be used instead of CHRST) CHRSTRP – Reciprocal of chair rise time multiplied by 100 (so that a high score indicates good performance) ((1/CHRSTF)100) CHRST53G *– Chair rise response rate and reasons 1 = valid score 888 = not interviewed in 1999 (at age 53y) 995 = unable to finish the test 996 = result not valid – took < 5 seconds 997 = unable 998 = unwilling 999 = unknown To distinguish between those unable to do the test and those for whom the information is missing for other reasons, 995 and 997 should be grouped as unable and 996, 998 and 999 as missing.

Amendment August 2011:

On comparing chair rise time at age 53y with chair rise time at age 60-64y, 16 participants with outlying values at age 53y were identified (all with very high values i.e. range 113 to 322). On checking the details of these participants and finding that their performance in other tests at age 53y were not particularly poor and that the chair rise times at 60-64y were comparable with chair rise times at age 53y, if the times at age 53y were divided by 10. It was thus decided that the times for these participants were likely to be missing a decimal point and so new versions of the variables were created in which those 16 people with times >80s had their times divided by 10. CHRSTFC – Chair rise time (seconds) (corrected Aug 2011) CHRSTRPC – Reciprocal of chair rise time multiplied by 100 (calculated using corrected time) (so that a high score indicates good performance) ((1/CHRSTFC)*100)

Standing balance

was measured as the longest time, up to a maximum of 30 seconds, that the cohort member could maintain a one-legged stance. This was performed twice, once with eyes open and once with eyes closed.

Raw variables LEGBP – Balance time (seconds) with eyes open LEGBC – Balance time (seconds) with eyes closed

Derived variables As most people were able to balance on one leg with their eyes open for 30 seconds analyses have been performed using the measure of standing balance with eyes closed.

LGLEGBC – Natural log of balance time with eyes closed (as LEGBC is right skewed) – those with a recorded balance time of 0 (i.e. LEGBC=0) coded as missing LGLEG53G – Standing balance with eyes closed response rate and reasons 1 = valid score 888 = not interviewed in 1999 (at age 53y) 993 = attempted but score not obtained 996 = unable 999 = not attempted To distinguish between those unable to do the test and those for whom the information is missing for other reasons, 993 and 996 should be grouped as unable and 999 as missing.

Grip strength

was measured in kilograms using an electronic handgrip dynamometer. Four measures were recorded (two for each hand).

Raw variables The raw variables used to create a grip strength measure are not included in the physical performance save file. The raw variables are: GRPL1 GRPL2 GRPR1 GRPR2 and DYNAM.

Derived variables The measure of grip strength used in analyses is the highest value from the maximum of four measures recorded. As there were problems suspected with one of the dynamometers all those whose grip strength was measured using this instrument are coded as missing in all derived variables.

GRIP53CF – Grip strength (kg) (highest value recorded) (if this variable is used in analyses those with values of 0, 888 and 999 must first be coded as missing) GRIP53X – Grip strength (kg) (highest value recorded) – same as GRIP53CF but with those people with a value of 0, 888 or 999 recoded to missing GRIP53G – Grip strength response rate and reasons 1 = valid score 888 = not interviewed in 1999 (at age 53y) 993 = result not valid i.e. < 2 or > 90 kg 996 = unable 997 = dynamometer 48 (potentially faulty dynamometer) 999 = not attempted To distinguish between those unable to do the test and those for whom the information is missing for other reasons, 996 should be grouped as unable and 993, 997 and 999 as missing.

Summary performance score

Derived variables In addition to considering the three physical performance measures as separate outcomes they have also been used to derive a summary measure of overall functional performance by Guralnik and colleagues.1 To create this summary measure, outcomes of each of the three performance tests were rescaled to a 0 to 1 scale.
Balance was rescaled by dividing the total time the stand with eyes closed was held by the maximum possible time, 30 seconds. People unable to hold the position for even a small amount of time were assigned a score of 0.
Rescaled chair rise time was calculated using the equation 1-(chair rise time/48.0 seconds), where 48.0 was the 99th percentile of time. People unable to rise from a chair 10 times and those taking longer than 48.0 seconds were assigned a score of 0.
Grip strength adjusted for height was divided by the sex-specific 99th percentile value of adjusted grip strength (0.2838 kg/cm for women and 0.4346 kg/cm for men). Women and men with grip strength greater than 0.2838 kg/cm and 0.4346 kg/cm, respectively, were assigned a score of 1 and those unable to do the test assigned a score of 0. The three rescaled performance scores were summed to create a normally distributed aggregate functional performance score with a range of 0 to 3.

CONTSUM – Summary performance score

Categorisations of contsum

CONTHIGH

** **0 = Summary performance score not in top 10% 1 = Summary performance score in top 10% of scores

CONTLOW

** **0 = Summary performance score not in bottom 10% 1 = Summary performance score in bottom 10% of scores

CONTMID

** **1 = Summary performance score in bottom 10% 2 = Summary performance score in middle 80% 3 = Summary performance score in top 10%

Leg power

was measured using the Nottingham Power Rig (NPR).2 Participants were seated on the NPR with arms folded and their inactive leg resting loosely on the floor. They were instructed to push as hard and fast as possible against a large pedal from a flexed knee position to almost full extension with the seat position adjusted for each individual to ensure this. Average power was calculated from the recorded terminal velocity of the flywheel assuming that acceleration was constant. Two practice attempts were followed by at least three maximal attempts with strong verbal encouragement from the nurse.

Raw variables The raw variables are: LGXU99 (Leg used for testing (0=Right, 1=Left)), LGXSD99 (Seat distance (cm)), LGXP199, LGXP299 (Leg power in 2 practice attempts), LGXT199 – LGXT799 (Leg power in real attempts (up to a maximum of 7 attempts)), LGXNT99 (Reason not tested 4=other), CLIN99 (clinic identifier))

Derived variables The measure of leg extensor power used in analyses is the highest value of the first three real attempts made (not all participants took more than 3 real attempts so results from later attempts cannot be used).

LGXMAX – Maximum leg extensor power (watts) (highest of 3 values recorded) CLINIC – Clinic identifier (valid values 4, 111, 153, 236, 944) CLIPAR – Attended a clinic in 1999 0 = No 1 = Yes LGXMAXG – Leg power response rate and reasons 1 = valid score 888 = not a participant in clinic visits in 1999 (at age 53y) 999 = attended clinic but no valid score
