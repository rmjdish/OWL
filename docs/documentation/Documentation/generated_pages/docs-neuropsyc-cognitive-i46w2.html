Overview

Documentation - I46P2_NeuroPsyc_Cognitive – Insight 46 wave 2

| Categories of variables*: (may be more than one) | Cognitive and neurological status of participants | | Summary of work undertaken | Decisions regarding the cognitive and neurological status of participants were undertaken as described below. |

Dementia

Dementia was based on all available data including self-reported diagnosis (diagnosis1, other1, diagnosis2, other2), cognitive performance (including Ldz, Wdz and Es), information provided by the informant (ad8_total) and study clinician opinion.

Mild cognitive impairment

Criteria for mild cognitive impairment were based on published criteria (Petersen, R. C. et al. (2013) ‘Mild cognitive impairment due to Alzheimer disease in the community.’, Annals of neurology, 74(2), pp. 199–208. doi: 10.1002/ana.23931). The principle of these criteria is that they require an absence of dementia AND evidence of objective cognitive impairment on one or more neuropsychological tests AND subjective cognitive concerns reported by the participant or an informant. For Insight 46 these criteria were operationalised as detailed below. No clinical evidence of dementia AND participant concern regarding their cognition (memory or cognitive difficulties more than other people the same age or if they felt they would seek medical attention regarding their difficulties) and/or informant concern regarding the participant’s cognition AND objective evidence of either an amnestic (Logical Memory delayed recall ≥ 1.5 SD below the mean) and/or non-amnestic deficit (Digit-Symbol Substitution score ≥ 1.5 SD below the mean). These cognitive tests were chosen for defining a cognitive deficit on the basis of their normal distribution across the entire cohort.

Participant concern was determined based on answering ‘yes’ to either or both of the following questions within the subjective cognitive decline questionnaire: “Do you perceive memory or cognitive difficulties more than other people the same age?” (peers) and “Would you ask a doctor about these difficulties?” (doctor)

Informant concern was assessed using the AD8 (ad8_total) (Galvin, J. E. et al. (2005) ‘The AD8: A brief informant interview to detect dementia’, Neurology, 65(4), pp. 559–564. doi: 10.1212/01.wnl.0000172958.95282.2a.) and was defined as an AD8 score =>2. Z-scores for Digit-Symbol (Wz) and Logical Memory Delayed Recall (Ldz) were calculated based on the mean and SD of the full Insight 46 sample and a z-score of <=-1.5 was classified as a low score indicating evidence of a cognitive deficit.

Major brain disorder

Major brain disorders were determined by a consensus panel including consultant neurologists and study clinicians who had reviewed the participants. Decisions were made based on self-reported diagnosis (diagnosis1, other1, diagnosis2, other2), radiological reads (infarction, haemorrhage, lesion, aneurysm, colloidCyst, hydrocephalus, sinusDisease, other, comments, flaggedForReview) and clinician opinion. Participants were classified as having a major brain disorder if they had a history of a clinical or radiological diagnosis of a significant neurological or psychiatric condition. These included but were not limited to: Neurodegenerative disorders Multiple sclerosis Epilepsy requiring active treatment Psychiatric disorder requiring hospital admission, anti-psychotic medication, or electroconvulsive therapy Major neurosurgery Clinical or radiological (cortical) stroke

Notes

Dementia and major brain disorders were defined based on phase 1 and phase 2 data, whereas mild cognitive impairment was determined using phase 2 data only.
