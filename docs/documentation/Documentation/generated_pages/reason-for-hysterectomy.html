Reason for hysterectomy

Women’s hysterectomy status has been ascertained from age 43 years onwards (see derived hysterectomy status variables and accompanying documentation). Information on reason for hysterectomy is also available. Where possible, this information on reason for hysterectomy has been ascertained from hospital records but where hospital records were not available reasons have been ascertained from self-reports (details below).

Important note: Reason for hysterectomy variables generated using only hospital record information and using only self-reported information have been included in this file but for most analyses the combined variable REASONZ (described below) should be used.

Hospital records To obtain information from hospital records cohort members were asked during home visits in adulthood to detail any hospital admissions. From this information, where consent was given, hospitals were contacted to verify these reports and asked to provide details from their records of the admissions. Procedures admitted for and the reasons for these were coded using Office of Population Censuses and Surveys (OPCS) and International Classification of Diseases (10th revision) codes, respectively, by a research nurse. Variables on reasons for hysterectomies performed up to age 43 years were then coded by Diana Kuh and these were updated to include information from hospital records up to age 53 years by Rachel Cooper. A reason for hysterectomy variable (REASONH) was then generated from this hospital record information with recodes included for those women who were identified as having gynaecological cancers from the ONS cancer registrations (after confirming that date of diagnosis corresponded with date of hysterectomy).

2005 hysterectomy questionnaire Of the 403 women who had been identified as having undergone hysterectomy by age 57y 150 were coded as having an unknown reason for hysterectomy when all hospital record data were considered (i.e. REASONH=99). Information was missing because not all women who had had a hysterectomy participated in the data collections during which permission was gained to access hospital records, not all women who participated in the relevant data collections consented to the hospitals being contacted, some hospitals contacted did not return any information and some women had their hysterectomies after 1999 when hospital records were last obtained.

To improve the completeness of information on reason for hysterectomy a self-completion postal questionnaire (see below) was designed and sent in 2005 to women who had undergone hysterectomy. A variable to identify those women sent this 2005 questionnaire (SENT) is available - there were 29 women who had undergone hysterectomy but who were no longer eligible to be contacted (either through death (n=15), permanent refusal to participate (n=3) or losses (n=11)). Of the 374 women who were sent a questionnaire 322 (86.1%) completed and returned it and a further 6 (1.6%) did not receive the questionnaire as they were returned undelivered.

From the information returned in postal questionnaires a reason for hysterectomy variable (REAS05M) was generated. The validity of these self-reported reasons was tested among those women who had both a hospital record and self-reported reason available (n=201) – see R Cooper, unpublished PhD thesis.

Combined reason for hysterectomy variable The self-reported reasons for hysterectomy were then combined with the hospital records information, taking the information from the hospital records in preference to the self-reported reason where both were available to create a final reason for hysterectomy variable (REASONZ). This variable should be used in analyses.
