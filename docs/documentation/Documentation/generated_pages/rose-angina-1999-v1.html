Rose Angina 1999

There are 3 scales for Rose Angina: stage 1, stage 2 and a combined variable.

In 1999 there was an earlier question which asked if the study member had ever been told by a doctor that that had had angina. Ninety people replied yes and these people were not asked the Rose Angina questions. These study members have been separated from the derived rose angina variables below.

Stage 1 Rose Angina

If Study member has ever had any pain or discomfort in their chest (ANGIN =1)

The all of the following conditions must be met:

The pain must be in the Sternum (upper and middle) or Sternum (lower), left anterior chest or the left arm; (CHPRL1 thru CHPRL5 at least one <=4)

They must get the pain when walking uphill or in a hurry but not when walking at an ordinary pace on the level; (CHPRU = 1 AND CHPRN = 2)

That pain is relieved in 10 minutes or less when they stand still. (CHPRS = 1 AND CHPRT = 1).

Stage 2 Angina

If Study member has ever had any pain or discomfort in their chest (ANGIN =1)

The all of the following conditions must be met:

The pain must be in the Sternum (upper and middle) or Sternum (lower), left anterior chest or the left arm; (CHPRL1 thru CHPRL5 at least one <=4)

They must get the pain when walking at an ordinary pace on the level; (CHPRN = 1)

That pain is relieved in 10 minutes or less when they stand still. (CHPRS = 1 AND CHPRT = 1).

Overall Rose Angina

This is the sum of stage 1 and 2.

Study members who did not answer the routing question (ever had any pain or discomfort in their chest) were coded as unknown unless they answered the rest of the battery of questions.

Study members who did not answer the walking uphill AND walking on the flat were also coded as ‘unknown’.

Otherwise respondents who answered most but not all of the questions were coded as ‘no’.
