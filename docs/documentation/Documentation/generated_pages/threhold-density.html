Threshold percent density readings

Isabel carried out threshold percent density readings on the left CCs (if available) for the 1946 study. were carried out on film copies, so skin sometimes not visible.

Threshold readings were made for 891 (1074 with duplicates) women. (It’s possible to include more women later if we do the MLO readings on them). Restricting to women included in the original paper (table below), we have a greater proportion of Wolfe grades DY that do not have percent density readings.

Distribution of percent density

A squared root transformation will be suitable (see histograms below). This is what most other studies have done too (Boyd etc).

As the square root transformation is not easily interpretable, normally I standardize this variable and then beta-coefficients from regression models can be interpreted as ‘number of standard deviations difference’.

I have used the commands:

gen sqrtpd1=sqrt(pd1) sum sqrtpd1 gen stdsqrtpd1=sqrtpd1/sqrt(_result(4))

Then run regression models: regress stdsqrtpd1 ….

Repeatability 10% of films were repeated within and between reading batches, to estimate the repeatability of readings. The intraclass correlation coefficient was 0.90 (95% CI 0.88, 0.93) , this is the ratio of the between-woman variance to the total variance (between-woman variance + variance due to measurement errors in reading).

Data If multiple readings on the same film were made (for repeatability), the average of these readings has been provided.
