Background to cognitive tests

Full details of these cognitive tests have been published in the following two papers: Lane et al. (2017) Study protocol: Insight 46 – a neuroscience sub-study of the MRC National Survey of Health and Development, BMC Neurology, 17:75, doi: 10.1186/s12883-017-0846-x Lu et al. (2019) Cognition at age 70: life course predictors and associations with brain pathologies, Neurology, doi: 10.1212/WNL.0000000000008534 A summary is provided below: The Mini Mental State Examination (MMSE) is a 30-point composite screening tool for cognitive impairment which is widely used within clinical practice (Folstein, M. F., Folstein, S. E. and McHugh, P. R. (1975) “Mini-mental state." A practical method for grading the cognitive state of patients for the clinician., Journal of Psychiatric Research, 12(3), pp. 189–198. doi: 10.1016/0022-3956(75)90026-6). The item-by-item scores are provided in this dataset, as well as the total score. Digit-Symbol Substitution from the Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale-Revised is an index of executive function and psychomotor speed (Wechsler D. (1981b) Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale - Revised. San Antonio, TX: The Psychological Corporation). The score is the number of items completed correctly within 90 seconds. Logical Memory IIa from the Wechsler Memory Scale assesses free recall of a short story, which the participant is asked to recall immediately and after a delay of approximately 20 minutes (Wechsler D. (1987) Wechsler Memory Scale - Revised: Manual. San Antonio, TX: Psychological Corporation). The maximum score is 25. The exact delay duration is recorded so that it can be accounted for in analyses. The Face-Name test (FNAME-12) assesses associative memory for face-name and face-occupation pairs (Papp, K. V et al. (2014) Development of a psychometrically equivalent short form of the Face-Name Associative Memory Exam for use along the early Alzheimer’s disease trajectory, Clin Neuropsychol. 2014/05/13, 28(5), pp. 771–785. doi:10.1080/13854046.2014.911351). Two versions exist: FNAME-12A and FNAME12-B. This study used FNAME-12A. Participants are shown 12 unfamiliar face-name and face-occupation pairs (e.g. “Sarah, Reporter”), with 8 seconds to study each one. They are then presented with each face and asked to recall the associated name and occupation. This process is repeated with a second learning phase and a second recall test. After a ~10-minute delay they are again shown each face and asked to recall the names and occupations (the third recall test). After a ~30-minute delay participants are shown 12 sets of three faces and asked to identify each previously learned face from the two distractors (facial recognition) and to recall the name and occupation (the fourth recall test). If they cannot recall the name and/or occupation, they are asked to select the correct answer from three options comprising: the correct answer, a distractor (a name/occupation that belongs with a different face in the set), and a name/occupation that did not feature in the set. The summary outcomes are FN-N (total names recalled, max. 48), FN-O (total occupations recalled, max. 48) and FNAME-total (FN-N + FN-O, max. 96) – these outcomes are based on the four recall tests. For Phase 2, we also administered an additional recall trial after 7 days, by telephone (or by video call for some participants assessed between Nov 2020 to Jan 2021). The procedure for this trial was the same as for the 30-minute delay described above. The Matrix Reasoning test assesses non-verbal reasoning, an aspect of fluid intelligence (Wechsler D. (1999) The Wechsler abbreviated scale of intelligence. San Antonio, TX: The Psychological Corporation). Participants are shown a matrix of geometric shapes and are required to select the missing piece from five options. There are 32 matrices, graded in difficulty, and the test is discontinued when participants make four consecutive errors (or four errors within five consecutive items), as specified in the manual. Note that the full test contains 35 items but the final 3 items are only to be administered to younger adults, as per the instructions in the manual, so were not used in Insight 46. Complex figure drawing is a test of visual memory recall from the Adult Memory and Information Processing Battery (AMIPB). The adult memory and information processing battery (AMIPB): test manual. Leeds: A K Coughlin, Psychology Dept, St James’ Hosp, 1985. Two versions exist: figure A and figure B. We used figure A. Participants are asked to copy a complex figure presented and then to draw it from memory three times: immediately, after a 30-minute delay, and after a 7-day delay. The 7-day drawing was administered by telephone (or by video call for some participants assessed between Nov 2020 to Jan 2021). Immediately after completing their 7-day drawing and sealing it in an envelope, they are given a recognition memory test with 4 trials; in each trial, they must identify the correct part of the original figure from among 3 options.

Data cleaning

For some tests, the tester made notes about issues that may be relevant to analysis e.g. minor deviations from the protocol. These were stored in new variables named (see bold text) and labelled as follows: Enotes MMSE: any notes on administration and data Fnotes FNAME: any notes on administration and data Wnotes Digit Symbol: any notes on administration and data Gnotes GNT: any notes on administration and data One participant did not complete the full FNAME test, and therefore her total scores (Ffnn, Ffno, Ftot) automatically generated in XNAT were not valid. These total scores were changed to missing and this was noted in the Fnotes variable.
Where data were missing and explanations were given, these explanations were tidied up to use the same wording for the same reason e.g. “remote telephone assessment” and “telephone remote assessment” were combined to a common phrase. For the MMSE, two participants were administered the ‘spell WORLD backwards’ item (Ebackwards) unnecessarily, as they had already scored full marks on the subtraction item (Esubtract_tot). Their scores for the Ebackwards item were changed to missing, for consistency with the other participants. This does not affect the MMSE total score. Missing data were coded as -99 – this applies to all values that were missing due to the participant not completing a certain measure, or not having useable data. The only exceptions are date variables (visit_date Adate_cid, Adate_7) where -99 in Stata gets displayed as 24/09/59, so missing data for these variables have been left as “.”. Where values were blank due to the variable not being applicable to that participant, they are coded as -88. For example: The variable Liwn gives the reason why the Logical Memory Immediate test was not completed, if it is recorded as not completed in the Licomp variable (so Liwn is not applicable for participants who did complete the test). In the MMSE, the second and third attempts at the registration item (repeating the names of three objects) are only administered if the first attempt is not correct, so these variables are not applicable to participants whose first attempt was correct.
The variable remote_type is not applicable to participants who did not have a remote assessment.

The Preclinical Alzheimer Cognitive Composite (PACC)

The four components of our version of the PACC were: MMSE total score, Logical Memory delayed recall score, Digit-Symbol Substitution score and FNAME-total. The four components were converted into z-scores based on the mean and standard deviation of the full Insight 46 sample at Phase 1, using the formula: z-score = (score - mean) / standard_deviation The four z-scores (Ez, Ldz, Wz, Ftotz) were then averaged. The Phase 1 means and standard deviations were used so that it will be possible to evaluate change on the PACC between the two time-points. A higher PACC score indicates better performance. Some participants did not have all four scores were available (missing data were mainly due to tests not being administered for remote assessments between Nov 2020 to Jan 2021). For these participants, the PACC was generated if they had at least two of the four tests. The number of tests available for each participant was recorded in a new variable called PACCnum. Researchers using these data should consider whether to include these participants in their analyses or not. Note that the original PACC (Donohue, M. C. et al. (2014) ‘The preclinical Alzheimer cognitive composite: measuring amyloid-related decline.’, JAMA neurology, 71(8), pp. 961–70. doi: 10.1001/jamaneurol.2014.803) contained the Free and Cued Selective Reminding Test (FCSRT) instead of FNAME. We replaced the FCSRT with FNAME to avoid potential overlap with a similar word-learning memory test administered to the NSHD cohort at multiple time-points throughout adulthood. Other cognitive measures were also transformed to z-scores, again based on the mean and standard deviation of the full Insight 46 sample at Phase 1.

Z-scores for defining Mild Cognitive Impairment

For defining Mild Cognitive Impairment, our criteria include objective evidence of either an amnestic (Logical Memory delayed recall ≥1.5 SD below the mean) or nonamnestic deficit (DSST score ≥1.5 SD below the mean). For this purpose, z-scores were calculated based on the Phase 2 mean and standard deviation, because we want to define low performance relative to participants’ current age. (A certain amount of age-related decline is expected since Phase 1.) These variables are named Ldz_mci and Wz_mci. Two variables were created to dichotomise these into low_logical_memory and low_digit_symbol, where 1=a low score (<=-1.5) and 0=a ‘normal’ score.

Additional AMIPB figure scores

For the AMIPB recognition test, variables were calculated to code whether each response (A, B, or C) was correct or incorrect for the four trials.

Arecog4

These were then summed to give a total correct out of 4 (Arecog).

Accelerated Forgetting scores

To evaluate Accelerated Forgetting, previous studies have calculated a score that is the proportion of material retained i.e. ‘score after 7 days’ divided by ‘score after 30 minutes’. This was calculated for the FNAME recall tests (names, occupations, total), FNAME multiple choice recognition tests (names, occupations, total), and AMIBP figure test, as follows: Fn_acc = Fcrn7 / Fcrn30 Fo_acc = Fcro7 / Fcro30 Ftot_acc (Fcrn7 + Fcro7) / (Fcrn30 + Fcro30) Fmcn_acc = Fmcn7 / Fmcn Fmco_acc = Fmco7 / Fmco Fmc_acc (Fmcn7 + Fmco7) / (Fmcn + Fmco) Aacc = A7/Ad
