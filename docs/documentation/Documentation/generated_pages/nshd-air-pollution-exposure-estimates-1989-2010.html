NSHD - Environmental Exposures - Metadata

The exposure data provided are the result of several years’ work from various research teams.

Before using any of the exposure estimates in any study we strongly advise that the planned

analyses are discussed with the relevant contacts (contact details are provided in the metadata).

Depending on the focus of the research and level of involvement, the authors should be

acknowledged in any publication or included as co-authors.

  1. Data

File: NSHD_AP_89.csv (Number of records: 5,362)

NSHD_AP_99.csv (Number of records: 5,362)

NSHD_AP_06-10.csv (Number of records: 4,923)

These files contain time relevant air pollution exposure estimates for NSHD participants at their

residential address for the 1989, 1999 and 2006-2010 follow-up (i.e. individuals are assigned air

pollution concentrations based on their residential address they lived at a specific time). Individual

fields and models used to derive the annual average ambient concentrations are described in detail

below.

  1. Air pollution estimates for the NSHD 1989 follow-up

NSHD_AP_89.csv

Annual average black smoke (BS), sulphur dioxide (SO2) and nitrogen dioxide (NO2) concentrations,

1991

Black smoke (BS), sulphur dioxide (SO2) and nitrogen dioxide (NO2) maps were modelled based on a

land use regression (LUR) model. BS & SO2 concentrations were modelled as part of the CHESS

project (Welcome Trust funded, grant 075883); the NO2 map is based on input data from the

Automatic Urban and Rural Network (AURN), Defra. For details of the models see references

Gulliver et al, 2011 (for BS & SO2) and Gulliver et al, 2013 (for NO2), citation below.

BS, SO2 and NO2 concentrations for the year 1991 were derived from GB-wide air pollution maps.

Resolution of BS and SO2 maps are 100m x 100m, for NO2 map 200m x 200m. The x,y-coordinates of

residential address of NSHD participants in 1989 were overlaid with these maps and the

corresponding air pollution concentration of the grid cell were assigned to the coordinate. 1991 is

the closest year to the 1989 follow-up for which such information is available.

  1. Air pollution estimates for the 1999 NSHD follow-up

NSHD_AP_99.csv

3.1 Back extrapolation of ESCAPE LUR model for nitrogen dioxide (NO2), nitrogen oxides (NOX) and

particulate matter (PM10) concentrations, 1999

These data have been supplied to NSHD previously for use in the LEAP and ESCAPE studies. First, air

pollution estimates for the year 2010 were modelled for the address each individual lived at in 1999

using Land Use Regression (LUR) models developed as part of the European Study of Cohorts for Air

Pollution Effects (ESCAPE) (http://www.escapeproject.eu/), funded under the EU 7th Framework

Programme. For details of the models see references Eeftens et al, 2012 and Beelen et al, 2013,

citations provided below and modelling of annual average air pollution concentrations, 2010 for the

2006-10 follow-up described in section 4 (page 5).

UK Small Area Health Statistics Unit – October 2014

3

Based on the modelled 2010 concentrations back-extrapolation to the year 1999 was performed

adjusting for time trends based on daily monitoring data from 1996 to 2011. The monitoring site

that had the most complete monitoring data across the periods was used for this purpose:

 ESCAPE reference site monitoring period – 16/03/2010 – 01/02/2011

 ECCAPE baseline – 2 years either side of 1 July 1999 (1 July 1998 – 30 June1999 and 1 July

1999 – 30 June 2000)

Back-extrapolation was only possible for NO2, NOX and PM10 and not for all ESCAPE air pollutants as

only these three pollutants had representative long-running monitoring sites suitable to use for the

back-extrapolation.

Concentrations for 1999 were calculated using both the absolute and the ratio method. The ratio

method was used for a published ESCAPE paper on respiratory disease using the NSHD (Cai, 2014)

because the spatial contrast in air pollutants was larger in the past and the few available previous

studies have used a ratio method (Slama et al, 2007).

Absolute: Cextrapolated = CESCAPE + Diffroutine

Ration: Cextrapolated = CESCAPE * Ratioroutine

Pollutant CRoutine_ESCAPE CRoutine_Baseline DiffRoutine RatioRoutine

NO2 11.1 14.0 2.9 14/11.1 = 1.27

NOx 13.1 18.6 5.5 18.6/13.1 = 1.42

PM10 14.9 18.2 3.3 18.2/14.9 = 1.22

For full details please see:

http://www.escapeproject.eu/manuals/Procedure_for_extrapolation_back_in_time.pdf

Please note that the ESCAPE estimates for PM10 are valid up to 400km from the monitoring area,

which was in Greater London, but it is unclear how good the estimates are outside this area. All

addresses which are more than 400km away from Greater London are not assigned a PM10 estimate

concentrations and have missing data (.) instead.

Please also note that if your study requires use of air pollution estimates for the 2006-10 follow-up

and additionally the 1999 follow-up we recommend not using ESCAPE for both time-points, i.e.

ESCAPE (for 2006-10, see section 4) together with back-extrapolated ESCAPE (for 1999, as here,

section 3.1) as these estimates are not independent of each other – back-extrapolated ESCAPE

estimates were derived from the original ESCAPE estimates. An alternative to back-extrapolated

ESCAPE estimates in this situation is the RGI estimates for 2001 (detailed below, section 3.2).

3.2 Nitrogen dioxide (NO2) and particulate matter (PM10) concentrations, 2001

NO2 and PM10 maps were modelled based on a land use regression (LUR) model as part of the RGI

project (grant RGI-137, Dutch program Ruimte voor Geoinformatie). For details of the model see

reference Vienneau et al., 2009, citation below.

NO2 and PM10 concentrations for the year 2001 were derived from GB-wide air pollution maps

(resolution 100m x 100m). X,y-coordinates of NSHD participants were overlaid with these maps and

the corresponding air pollution concentration of the 100m x 100m grid cell were assigned to the

coordinate. 2001 is the closest year to the 1999 follow-up for which such information is available.

  2. Air pollution estimates for the 2006-2010 NSHD follow-up

NSHD_AP_06-10.csv

Annual average air pollution concentrations, 2010 (NO2, NOX, PM2.5, PM2.5 absorbance, PM10 and

PMCOARSE)

UK Small Area Health Statistics Unit – October 2014

6

Annual average concentrations for the year 2010 were modelled for each individual’s address during

the 2006-10 follow-up using a Land Use Regression (LUR) model developed as part of the European

Study of Cohorts for Air Pollution Effects (ESCAPE) (http://www.escapeproject.eu/), funded under

the EU 7th Framework Programme. For details of the models see references Eeftens et al, 2012 and

Beelen et al 2013, citations provided below.

Land Use Regression (LUR) was used to estimate ambient air pollution concentrations at the home

addresses of the NSHD participants. LUR combines the actual measurement of air pollution done

between 26 Jan 2010 - 18 January 2011 and the development of stochastic models using predictor

variables such as traffic variables, population density and meteorology derived with Geographical

Information Systems (GIS) to develop statistical models using linear regression. The model is then

applied to a large number of unsampled locations in the study area. This technique is widely used in

epidemiological research to investigate possible links between air pollution and health effects (for a

review of LUR models to assess air pollution, see Hoek et al., 2008).

Air pollution estimates are representative for the year 2010. Traffic variables were calculated during

the course of LUR.

Please note that the ESCAPE estimates for PM10, PM2.5, PM2.5 absorbance and PM coarse are valid up

to 400km from the monitoring area, which was in Greater London, but it is unclear how good the

estimates are outside this area. All addresses which are more than 400km away from Greater

London are not assigned a PM10 estimate concentrations and have missing data (.) instead.

Please note that some NSHD participant changed address around the 2006-2010 follow-up period

which results in multiple entries of individuals (ESCAPE_ID) because air pollution 2010 annual

average air pollution concentrations were modelled for all addresses a NSHD participant lived at

between 2004 and 2011.
