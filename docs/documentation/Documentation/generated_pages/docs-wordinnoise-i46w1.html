Overview

Documentation – I46_WordInNoise – Insight46 wave 1 For Submission to the NSHD Scientific Support Team | Categories of variables*: (may be more than one) | Word in noise | | Summary of work undertaken | Individual .csv Word in noise files amalgamated into one spreadsheet Participants who did not undergo testing values coded as -99 (in each case this was due to functioning testing equipment not being available on the day) All individual .csv files were checked all fields were completed and labelled with correct participant number by TP in an ongoing manner throughout testing Please see methods section below for more details of testing |

357 out of the 368 participants analysed underwent two trials of a word-in-multi-talker babble identification paradigm whereby high frequency monosyllabic words were presented in a random order with a multi-talker babble (composed of 20 voices presented at a fixed level of 65 dB SPL) in the background The sound level of each individual word varied with each presentation as defined by a signal to noise ratio (SNR). After each word the participant was asked to repeat verbatim what they heard. The experimenter then recorded the response as correct or incorrect. Each trial commenced with an SNR of 12 dB and tracked 50% correct adaptively with a one-up one-down rule (maximum SNR 18 dB). The test ended after 8 reversals or 25 trials, with the Speech Reception Threshold (SRT: the SNR that leads to approximately 50% correct answers) calculated from the mean of the reversals. To rule out possible contributions of differences in audiometric thresholds above 4 kHz, the stimuli were low-pass filtered at 4 kHz using a 4th order Butterworth filter. Eight different lists of 25 highly frequency monosyllabic words were available and were chosen at random for each trial. No one list was used more than once for each participant. In 129 out of 140 participants with evidence of hearing impairment (PTA best hearing ear > 25 dB HL) two additional trials were performed where the stimuli were spectrally shaped using the National Acoustics Laboratories-Revised (NAL-R) linear prescriptive formula based on their individual thresholds (Byrne and Dillon, 1986; Schoof and Rosen, 2014). The order in which these trials were performed relative to the non-NAL-R assisted trials was random for each subject to exclude the possibility of learning effects influencing the results. The participants were not informed of the change in nature of the auditory stimuli if a NAL-R formula had been applied. The participants were seated in a quiet room and stimuli were presented binaurally over ATH-M50 professional studio monitor headphones via MATLAB software on a Lenovo Yoga Ultrabook with Intel inside CORE i7 processer with Focusrite Scarlett 2i2 external USB soundcard. Participants were instructed to keep their eyes open throughout the testing.
